### [Essay argues against using AI for writing, sparking HN debate](https://erichgrunewald.substack.com/p/why-you-should-almost-never-use-ai) ⭐️ 7.0/10

An essay on Erich Grunewald&\#x27;s Substack argues that people should almost never use AI to write, prompting Hacker News discussion about when LLMs are appropriate. Citing Eric Schwitzgebel, it says there is a large cognitive difference between passively reading text and actively generating prose, because once AI text is on the page writers may accept approximate wording instead of making effortful word choices. Commenters largely agree LLMs can help with private tasks such as summarizing research, analyzing data, drafting an email from a meeting transcript, or critiquing one&\#x27;s own writing, while arguing they should not produce text meant for others to consume. Several practitioners add that AI prose can be vague and wrong in hard-to-notice ways, requiring costly re-reading and correction, and that LLM critiques tend to demand full rewrites that writers must resist.

hackernews · erwald · Sep 19, 16:35 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49767937)

**「Background」** The item is an argumentative essay by Erich Grunewald arguing that AI should almost never be used to write substantive text, while allowing uses such as line and copy editing or rewriting a passage for clarity if a human deliberately accepts or rejects each edit. Grunewald&\#x27;s blog frames the same argument as applying specifically to anything substantive. This position sits within broader debates about LLM-assisted writing and where to draw the line between acceptable editing support and substituting AI-generated prose for one&\#x27;s own.

**「Impact」** The practical consequence for writers and knowledge workers is a sharper boundary between using LLMs for private synthesis or critique and using them to generate prose for external readers, where the discussion reports correction costs and lost nuance.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters generally favor LLMs for summarization, data analysis, transcript-to-email drafting, and critique of one&\#x27;s own writing, but caution against letting them write text intended for other people. Reported drawbacks include buried meaning, subtle errors and vagueness, and overreaching rewrite suggestions; one commenter argues publishing the prompt itself would often communicate more effectively.

### [California Governor Signs Order Proposing Mandatory AI Loss-of-Control Reporting](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/usstock/c/2026-09-19/doc-inisisqc3124180.shtml) ⭐️ 7.0/10

California Governor Gavin Newsom signed an executive order on September 19 to strengthen AI safety, proposing that companies be required to report &quot;loss-of-control&quot; incidents involving AI agents and that advanced models potentially carry emergency shutdown mechanisms. The order convenes a panel of experts to issue guidance within two months on improving AI safety legislation, and also proposes periodic audits of AI laboratories. Newsom said federal regulation is insufficient and that California therefore needs to act on its own. The measures remain proposals that depend on the expert panel rather than finalized requirements.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 19, 05:44

**「Background」** California is already a focal point for frontier-AI regulation: in 2024 Governor Gavin Newsom vetoed SB 1047, a bill meant to mitigate catastrophic harms from highly advanced models, and then convened a Joint California AI Policy Working Group whose recommendations guided SB 53, the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act, which targets so-called catastrophic risk. The new executive order extends that trajectory by advancing ideas such as requiring independent third parties to write safety plans for frontier AI companies and mandating an emergency shutoff, or &quot;kill switch,&quot; for frontier models. Its proposals remain subject to an expert panel rather than being immediate legal requirements.

**「Impact」** California AI developers and labs could face new incident-reporting, independent safety evaluation, third-party verification, and emergency-shutdown testing expectations, though state officials said the order does not immediately require companies to install a kill switch. Those measures are still recommendations to be developed by state agencies and an expert panel, so they are not yet binding requirements.

### [Apple exec addresses iPhone Duo crease and hinge design](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/19/apple-exec-iphone-crease/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Apple hardware engineering vice president Tom Marieb said the foldable iPhone Duo uses a matte nano-texture display to reduce reflections and make the crease less visible, and he said he wants users to put Apple to the test on crease performance. He also said the iPhone Duo hinge was tuned over a long period so that opening and closing feels like a high-end car door, and that it supports half-fold stand, open, and closed positions. The device is set for preorders on October 16 and sales on October 23, starting at $1,999. The report is a short secondary account and has not been independently verified.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 19, 06:36

**「Background」** Foldable smartphones have long faced scrutiny over the visible crease that can form along the folding display and over hinge durability, issues that Apple is addressing with its first foldable, the iPhone Duo. According to reports on the launch, Apple hardware engineering vice president Tom Marieb said the company focused on the hinge, display finish, and long-term feel, with a matte nano-texture display intended to reduce glare and make the crease less visible. The iPhone Duo is positioned as Apple’s entry into the foldable category, with preorders on October 16 and sales on October 23 starting at $1,999.

**「Impact」** At a $1,999 starting price with October 16 preorders and an October 23 release, crease visibility and hinge feel become the primary trade-offs buyers must weigh at the top of Apple&\#x27;s iPhone lineup. The dates, price, and hardware claims rest on a short, unverified secondary report, and earlier supply-chain coverage described yield failures during foldable validation testing, so actual availability remains uncertain.

### [Beijing Regulator Investigates Meituan, Fliggy, Tongcheng and Tujia Over Hotel Algorithm Marketing](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/FsHQ-AG2zSNSWfA2sJAXfQ) ⭐️ 7.0/10

On September 19, Beijing&\#x27;s market regulator opened an investigation into Meituan, Fliggy, Tongcheng and Tujia over suspected violations in their hotel-accommodation businesses, including requiring bidding to win traffic placement and obliging merchants to sell at the lowest price across all platforms, which the regulator says strips them of pricing autonomy. The four companies said they are cooperating with the investigation; no findings or penalties have been announced yet.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 19, 07:47

**「Background」** Beijing&\#x27;s market regulator had already sent inspection teams into Meituan, Fliggy, Tongcheng and Tujia in April this year, reviewing their algorithm, marketing and legal operations and collecting data, and the September 19 filing turns that inquiry into a formal case. Ctrip and Qunar are not named in this investigation, which the report attributes to Ctrip&\#x27;s earlier antitrust case having already been investigated and penalized.

**「Impact」** Hotel operators selling rooms through these platforms are the group most directly affected, because the investigation targets practices such as requiring merchants to sell at the lowest price across all sites and to bid for prominent placement in search results — practices that constrain how hotels set rates and reach customers, though any change depends on findings the regulator has not yet issued.
