OpenAI published the first test results for Jalapeño, its custom inference ASIC developed with Broadcom. Compared with Nvidia&\#x27;s GB300, Jalapeño delivered 1.5-1.9x more AI work per unit of power at peak throughput, 1.7-3.6x lower end-to-end latency, and 2.1-4.1x better performance in high-interaction scenarios across GPT-OSS 120B, DeepSeek R1 670B, and Kimi K2.5 1T workloads. The chip is rated at 700W but sustained no more than 550W in tests, and it is not designed for model training or benchmarked against Nvidia&\#x27;s just-shipping Vera Rubin. OpenAI plans to deploy Jalapeño in its own compute facilities before the end of the year, with a second generation in deep development and a third in design. The results, if independently verified, could reshape inference hardware economics, but they remain vendor-controlled claims for now.

**「Background」** Custom ASICs execute fixed workloads more efficiently than general-purpose GPUs, but historically lacked flexibility and were expensive to design. OpenAI&\#x27;s move is an effort to reduce dependence on Nvidia and cut inference costs at scale, while Nvidia continues to dominate the AI accelerator market with products such as GB300 and Vera Rubin.

**「Impact」** For OpenAI, Jalapeño promises materially lower inference cost and power per token in its own datacenters, and it intensifies competitive pressure on Nvidia in inference-specific silicon. The immediate effect is limited because the chip is not for training and is not yet deployed at scale, so real-world TCO remains unproven.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters drew analogies to the early 3dfx/Riva/PowerVR GPU era and debated whether OpenAI could eventually bake model weights into future chips, while another noted that human brains still appear about 22x more efficient on a token/Joule basis. Several saw the choice of DeepSeek and Kimi benchmarks as a sign of where inference demand is heading, though one asked whether these chips will make GPUs affordable for individuals.
