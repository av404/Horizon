### [OpenAI Claims Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem Solved](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wavdi7/openal_says_it_has_cracked_one_of_maths/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A Reddit post relayed New York Times and OpenAI announcements claiming that OpenAI has produced a solution to the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems. The purported proof, attributed to an internal OpenAI system, reportedly shows that the Navier–Stokes fluid dynamics equations can develop a singularity in finite time. If substantiated, the result would be a landmark achievement in AI-assisted mathematics, but it has not yet undergone independent peer review or verification by the broader mathematics community. The post links to an official OpenAI announcement and the New York Times article, underscoring the timely nature of the claim despite remaining uncertainty about its validity.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/Shizuka\_Kuze · Sep 8, 17:42

**「Background」** The Navier–Stokes equations describe fluid motion, and the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem — one of the Clay Mathematics Institute’s seven Millennium Prize Problems, each carrying a $1 million prize — asks whether these equations can develop a singularity, or “blow up,” in finite time from smooth initial conditions. OpenAI announced that one of its internal systems has produced a proof that such a finite-time singularity is possible, including both a written writeup and a formal proof in the Lean proof assistant, using a multi-agent setup that reportedly involved about 10,000 subagents at one point. The result is not yet independently verified and has already drawn dispute from rival researchers, while mathematician Terence Tao has also warned that AI-driven races on open problems could inhibit normal mathematical collaboration.

**「Impact」** The most concrete consequence is that mathematicians and fluid dynamicists now face an unverified, high-stakes claim that the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem has been resolved in the negative, creating immediate pressure to audit the proof even as the announcement is already linked to accusations of unfair practice against an NYU mathematician. If independently confirmed, the claim would overturn the traditional framework used to model fluid motion by asserting that the equations can develop finite-time singularities and are fundamentally flawed.

**「Community Discussion」** Comments express both concern and skepticism: some highlight Terence Tao&\#x27;s warning that rumors of work on a problem can trigger a flood of AI-assisted effort and discourage sharing promising research directions, while others point to allegations that the result was based on someone else&\#x27;s unpublished work. There is also astonishment at the claim that an internal OpenAI model trained for less than two weeks is more than twice as capable in mathematics than another recently released model, though this detail remains unverified community commentary.

### [NeurIPS rejects 178 papers using flawed AI detector](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wakf62/neurips_deskrejected_178_papers_for_being/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

NeurIPS&\#x27;s 2026 Position Paper Track desk-rejected 178 submissions \(18.4% of the track\) based on the proprietary Pangram AI detector, with no human review or appeal process. Tests on recent papers by the three track chairs produced AI scores of 24%–69%, meaning they would have been at risk under the same enforcement rules. Pangram&\#x27;s default threshold initially flagged 42.7% of all submissions, and organizers reportedly shrank the text windows to lower the flag rate to 12.7%. The post also cites a Stanford study finding that 61.22% of human-written TOEFL essays are falsely flagged as AI, raising concerns about bias against non-native English authors, while NeurIPS published no demographic calibration data. The incident highlights the danger of using black-box AI detection without human review, especially since rejected authors have no appeal path and were told to simply resubmit elsewhere.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/tughanbulut · Sep 8, 10:19

**「Background」** The NeurIPS 2026 Position Paper Track used the commercial AI-writing detector Pangram 3.3.2 to screen all submissions and desk-rejected 178 papers \(18.4% of the track\) for suspected AI authorship, with no standard appeal path. According to the official NeurIPS blog, a Pangram score of 100% was interpreted not as “100% of the text is AI-generated” but as substantive AI use in many parts of the text, and a preliminary investigation found that 273 of 969 submissions \(28.2%\) received a score of 100%. The track chairs later published a detailed account of the screening process on the official NeurIPS blog, and independent researchers report that the chairs&\#x27; own papers would have been flagged by the same detector.

**「Impact」** For the 178 affected authors, the rejection is not a misconduct mark but is permanent for this NeurIPS track, with no appeal or blacklist removal process; they may resubmit to other venues such as ICLR or ICML. For future NeurIPS submitters, especially non-native English speakers, the lack of calibration data means uncertain but potentially significant risk of false AI detection.

### [Terence Tao warns AI research may deplete open math problems](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/9/terence-tao/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Terence Tao, in a Mathstodon post quoted by Simon Willison, warns that AI-powered research is mining the supply of good, fruitful open problems in a non-renewable fashion, potentially making such problems scarce. Tao says even the rumor of someone working on a problem can now trigger a massive amount of AI-powered effort to resolve it before the original research project reaches its full potential. This could shift incentives away from sharing promising research directions, reversing centuries of open-science tradition and causing serious long-term damage to the field. The commentary, republished by Willison, highlights a growing concern about how AI tools may reshape research culture in mathematics.

rss · Simon Willison · Sep 9, 00:20

**「Background」** Terence Tao, a Fields Medal-winning mathematician whose work spans number theory, harmonic analysis, and partial differential equations, has noted that mathematicians historically accept problems remaining open for decades or centuries. In recent commentary, he argues that AI-powered research can now rapidly solve or &quot;flatten&quot; open problems once they are mentioned, shifting longstanding incentives around sharing promising research directions and threatening the tradition of open science in mathematics.

### [ASML and TSMC Partner on High NA EUV 12-Inch Photomask Roadmap](https://www.nrc.nl/nieuws/2026/09/08/asml-gaat-samenwerken-met-taiwanese-chipgigant-tsmc-om-zijn-nieuwste-chipmachines-te-upgraden-a4936073) ⭐️ 7.0/10

ASML and TSMC announced on September 7 an industry partnership to shift High NA EUV lithography from today&\#x27;s 6-inch photomasks to a 12-inch format, targeting higher equipment productivity, lower chip manufacturing costs, and fewer stitching limitations. Under the plan, a 12-inch photomask pilot line is scheduled for 2031, with related systems aimed at advanced-node volume production in 2033. TSMC separately plans to use High NA EUV for advanced-node high-volume manufacturing starting in 2030. The roadmap gives chipmakers and suppliers a concrete timeline for replacing a key optical lithography component in future generations.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 8, 06:55

**「Background」** High NA EUV is ASML&\#x27;s next-generation extreme ultraviolet lithography system, designed to print smaller features at advanced chip nodes. In current lithography, the pattern is defined on a 6-inch photomask \(reticle\); moving to a 12-inch mask allows more chip patterns to be exposed in one pass, potentially increasing scanner productivity, lowering chip production costs, and removing the need for stitching multiple exposures. TSMC has also said it expects to use High NA EUV with existing 6-inch masks for high-volume manufacturing at advanced nodes from 2030, while ASML and partners are planning a 12-inch mask pilot line for 2031.

**「Impact」** For advanced chipmakers, the ASML-TSMC initiative sets an industry roadmap to move High NA EUV from 6-inch to 12-inch photomasks, which the companies say will further increase fab productivity, lower chipmaking costs, and remove stitching constraints.
